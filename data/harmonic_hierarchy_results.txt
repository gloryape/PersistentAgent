─── MATHEMATICAL EXPLANATION ───
  The two-harmonic resonance function is:
    res(θ) = [Σᵢ wᵢaᵢcos(θ-θᵢ) + γ₂·Σᵢ wᵢaᵢcos(2(θ-θᵢ))] / [(1+γ₂)W]

  Expanding both cosines:
    cos(θ-θᵢ)   = cos(θ)cos(θᵢ) + sin(θ)sin(θᵢ)
    cos(2(θ-θᵢ)) = cos(2θ)cos(2θᵢ) + sin(2θ)sin(2θᵢ)

  So: res(θ) = [cos(θ)·C₁ + sin(θ)·S₁ + γ₂(cos(2θ)·C₂ + sin(2θ)·S₂)] / [(1+γ₂)W]

  The d=2 statistic (C₁/W, S₁/W) captures only the first two terms.
  The d=4 statistic (C₁/W, S₁/W, C₂/W, S₂/W) captures all four.

  Each harmonic k adds TWO dimensions to the sufficient statistic:
    (Cₖ/W, Sₖ/W) = (Σ wᵢaᵢcos(kθᵢ)/W, Σ wᵢaᵢsin(kθᵢ)/W)

  For K harmonics: d = 2K. For K → ∞: d → ∞.
  This is the Fourier decomposition of the phase history distribution.
  A kernel sensitive to all Fourier modes requires the complete
  distribution — no finite summary suffices.
